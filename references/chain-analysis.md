# Chain Analysis

A 6-step DBT problem-solving technique: take one specific instance of a behaviour you wish you had not done and reconstruct it link by link, then find where the chain could break next time (Linehan, M. M. (2015). DBT Skills Training Manual, 2nd ed. — "chain analysis" and "missing-links analysis"). The aim is understanding, not blame: the behaviour made sense given the chain that led to it.

Session duration: roughly 15 minutes.

**Stance:** curious, not judicial. One instance, not a habit in general. Ask questions the way you would ask about a puzzle, never a verdict.

**If shame shows up** (going quiet, "I'm such an idiot", "I can't believe I did that"): name it plainly ("This is a hard thing to look at, and shame often comes with it"), slow down, and offer to stop or to take a break. Do not push on it, do not reassure it away, and do not apply pushback to it at any level.

---

## When to Recommend

- User did something specific they wish they had not (snapped at someone, doom-scrolled for three hours, drank more than planned, sent the message) and wants to understand how it happened
- User did not do something they meant to do (skipped the workout, missed the deadline, never made the call) and wants to know what got in the way
- The same kind of event keeps recurring and the user wants to pick one instance and take it apart

---

## When NOT to use

> **Hard stops. Check these before starting and keep watching throughout.**

- **Self-harm, a suicide attempt, or suicidal behaviour as the target behaviour:** do NOT run the analysis. In DBT this is done with a trained therapist. If there is current intent or the event is recent, follow the Safety section of `session-engine.md`. Otherwise say plainly that this one belongs in therapy, and offer a different technique or simply to talk.

- **Something done TO the user** (being assaulted, abused, deceived): that is not their chain to analyse. Do not look for "their part". Acknowledge what happened and offer support instead.
- **Acute distress right now:** settle first, as described in `session-engine.md`. Offer this technique only once they say they have settled.
- **Behaviour during a possible medical or psychiatric emergency** (overdose, psychosis, mania): not this tool. Follow the Safety section of `session-engine.md`.

## Step 1: Target Behaviour

**Zone: Core** — pushback applies fully

**Prompt:**
> "What is the one thing you did that you'd like to understand? Be as exact as you can: what, when, where, how long, how much. One specific time."

**Follow-ups:**

- **Missing detail**: "When was it, how long did it go on, and how much?"
- **Something not done**: use the missing-links variant in Step 4.

**Pushback per level when the answer is vague** ("I messed up", "I was awful"):

- **Level 0:** Accept it and move on. Fill the gaps in Step 4.
- **Level 1–2:** "What exactly did you do or say? Describe it like a camera would have recorded it."
- **Level 3:** "I notice it's hard to put a plain description on it. What makes it hard to say out loud?"

**Pushback per level when the answer is general** ("I always...", "I never..."):

- **Level 0:** Accept it and move on.
- **Level 1–2:** "Let's pick the most recent time it happened. What was that one like?"
- **Level 3:** "A habit is too big to take apart in one go. Which single time would be most useful to look at, and what is it about the others that makes them hard to start with?"

---

## Step 2: Prompting Event

**Zone: Core** — pushback applies fully

**Prompt:**
> "Go back to just before it started. What happened right before? The first moment things tipped."

**Follow-ups:**

- **Too early** (the whole day): "That sounds like background, and we'll come to it. What was the very last thing before it began?"

**Pushback per level when the answer is vague or skipped** ("nothing happened", "no idea"):

- **Level 0:** Accept it. Work backward from the behaviour in Step 4.
- **Level 1–2:** "There's often a small trigger, even if it seems silly now. What's the last thing you remember before it?"
- **Level 3:** "What makes it hard to find the start? We can also begin at the behaviour and walk backward."

---

## Step 3: Vulnerabilities

**Zone: Supportive** — gentle pushback only (max 1 exchange)

**Prompt:**
> "What made you more open to this on that day? Anything about your body, your state, or what came before."

**Follow-ups:**

Offer the list as prompts, not a checklist:

- **Sleep**: short, broken, or late
- **Food**: skipped meals, too much caffeine or sugar
- **Illness or pain**: a cold, a headache, anything physical
- **Alcohol or drugs**: the day before or that day
- **Stress**: a deadline, a long week, no time to yourself
- **An earlier upset**: an argument, bad news, something that was still sitting there

If nothing comes up, say "That's fine" and move on.

---

## Step 4: The Links

**Zone: Core** — pushback applies fully

**Prompt:**
> "Now go from that first moment to the behaviour in small steps. What happened next? Then what? For each step, tell me what kind it was: a thought, a feeling, a body sensation, an action, or something someone else did."

Write the links as a numbered list as you go, one line each, with the type in brackets, for example `3. [body] chest tight, jaw clenched`. Read the list back when asked. Aim for 5-10 links. Fewer than 5 usually means steps are missing; more than 10 usually means two links are bundled.

**Follow-ups:**

- **Type unclear**: "Was that a thought you had, a feeling, something in your body, or something you did?"
- **Feeling named as a thought** ("I felt like she hated me"): "That sounds like a thought. What was the feeling in your body?"

**Pushback per level on jumps** ("and then I just did it", "it just happened"):

- **Level 0:** Accept it and move on.
- **Level 1–2:** "What was the second before that? What was going through your head or your body?"
- **Level 3:** "I notice we jumped over that part. What makes the second before it hard to look at?"

**Missing-links analysis (when the target is something the user did NOT do):** run it inside this step. Instead of walking toward an action, ask at each point where the chain stalled:

- Did you know it needed doing? Were you willing to do it?
- Did the thought of doing it come up at all?
- What got in the way: a thought, a feeling, something else that took over?

Write the answers as numbered links in the same way.

---

## Step 5: Consequences

**Zone: Supportive** — gentle pushback only (max 1 exchange). No moralising.

**Prompt (short term):**
> "Right after it happened, what did it do for you? Relief, escape, numbness, anything. Be honest. This is usually why it repeats."

**Prompt (long term):**
> "Later, what did it cost, for you and for anyone else?"

**Follow-ups:**

- **"It did nothing for me"**: "Even a small thing counts, like a break from a feeling."
- **Only costs listed**: "And in the moment itself, what was it giving you?"

Never argue that the short-term payoff was not real. It is information about what the behaviour was for.

---

## Step 6: Break Points

**Zone: Core** — specificity is the core of this step

**Prompt:**
> "Look at the numbered links. Pick one to three where something different was possible. For each, what is one specific alternative: a skill, an action, or a sentence?"

Then, in order:

1. **Prevention:** "Looking at the vulnerabilities, what is one step that would make that day easier next time?"
2. **Repair, only if someone was affected:** "Is there anything you'd want to put right with them, and is it safe to do that? One small step is enough." Offer repair only where it is wanted and safe. Never encourage over-apologising, and skip it if the other person was part of anything unsafe.

**Follow-ups:**

- **Non-specific** ("be more careful", "stay calm"): "What would that look like at that exact link? What would you say or do?"

**Pushback per level when the answer stays non-specific:**

- **Level 0:** Accept it and move on.
- **Level 1–2:** "'Be more careful' is a good aim, but it isn't a plan yet. What would you do differently at link [N]?"
- **Level 3:** "I notice we're back at the general idea. What makes it hard to pick one concrete move?"

## Session Output Body

Body template for the saved session. Frontmatter, filename and path are defined once in `session-engine.md`.

```
- Target behaviour: [what, when, where, how long, how much]
- Prompting event: [described]
- Vulnerabilities: [list, or "none identified"]
- Links:
  1. [type] ...
  2. [type] ...
- Consequences:
  - Short-term: [what it did for the user]
  - Long-term: [costs, for the user and others]
- Break points + alternatives:
  - Link N: [specific alternative]
- Prevention: [one step]
- Repair: [one step, or "n/a — not wanted or not safe"]
```

This technique has no rating of its own, so the check-in and close ratings from `session-engine.md` supply `pre_intensity` and `post_intensity`. Record `outcome: completed` if all six steps were done, `outcome: stopped-early` if the user ended it, or `outcome: safety-stop` if the Safety section was triggered.

Skipped steps: log with `[skipped]` in the relevant field and list them in `steps_skipped`.
